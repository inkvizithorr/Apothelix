MORNING

Mornings set the whole day.

Your body runs on an internal clock. In the morning, you tell it the day has begun.

1

Daylight within half an hour

Open a window or step outside, even when it's cloudy. There's far more light outdoors than under a lamp.

2

A glass of water before coffee

After a night without a drink, it's the easiest first step. Enjoy your coffee, just not instead of breakfast.

3

Breakfast with protein

Eggs, quark, yoghurt or a bean spread. A sweet pastry on its own won't keep you going for long.

4

Wake up at the same time

Even at weekends, no more than an hour later. For your internal clock, regularity matters more than the length of one night.

Why mornings: miss the daylight and breakfast, and you often spend the rest of the day catching up on caffeine and sugar. A tidy morning costs less than afternoon patch-ups.

AFTERNOON

The afternoon makes or breaks the day.

After lunch comes a natural dip. You can't switch it off, but you don't have to add fuel to the fire.

5

A lunch you can work after

Half a plate of vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter sides. Overeating is the surest way to feel sleepy.

6

Ten minutes' walk after eating

Head outside, take the stairs, take a call standing up. A short move after lunch does more than another coffee.

7

Last coffee by 2 pm

Caffeine stays in your body for a long time. An afternoon espresso can still make itself felt at bedtime.

8

Water on your desk, not in the kitchen

A bottle within reach gets drunk. A glass in the kitchen waits until evening.

Tip: book a five-minute afternoon break in your calendar like a meeting. No one else will see it, but you will.

EVENING

Evenings close the day.

Sleep doesn't start in bed. It starts two hours before.

9

Dinner two to three hours before bed

Lighter than lunch. Falling asleep is harder on a full stomach.

10

An hour without screens

Phone out of the bedroom, charger in the living room. A book, a shower, a stretch, anything without notifications.

11

A cooler bedroom

Around 18 to 19 °C, dark and quiet. Air the room while you brush your teeth.

12

Empty your head onto paper

Write three things for tomorrow on a note. What's on paper doesn't need repeating at night.

Adults usually need 7 to 9 hours of sleep. At least 150 minutes of activity a week, even split into walks, is part of the WHO recommendations.
